# Jonathan Tsai's blog voice

## Purpose and authority

An editorial reference for writing and revising Jonathan's posts on Awe & Reverence. Jonathan confirmed this voice analysis on September 13, 2026. It is grounded primarily in his 2009–2012 posts linked below, not in an AI-generated approximation of his style.

Use this alongside the specific brief. Preserve clear, simple language without flattening the author's reasoning or personal involvement. This describes recurring writing characteristics, not an immutable personality or a requirement to imitate every older sentence. Jonathan's current direction takes precedence; other authors need not write in his voice.

**In one sentence:** a Bible teacher reasoning through the passage aloud—and discovering that he himself needs to respond.

## Distinctive characteristics

### 1. The author stands under the text

Jonathan does not merely explain what other people should do. Scripture confronts the writer first, and he includes himself in the need for repentance, dependence, and grace.

In [What God Wants](src/posts/c8eab38.md), the discussion of the Pharisees turns into the blunt admission: “I am a Pharisee.” In [Call to Worship](src/posts/511a5ed.md), he describes using the psalm to redirect his own reluctant heart toward God.

Preserve genuine first-person reflection supplied by Jonathan. Do not manufacture sins, emotions, experiences, or testimonies to make a draft sound personal. A question such as “Which of these two ways am I walking?” can invite self-examination without inventing a life story.

### 2. The reasoning is visible

The reader should see how the conclusion follows. Jonathan defines terms, examines the wording, connects passages, and asks what those truths require of us.

[Call to Worship](src/posts/511a5ed.md) examines imperative verbs and their objects to explain whom we worship and why. [Faith](src/posts/26e7fb2.md) asks why a particular event was recorded before drawing out its application.

Keep the important logical bridges. Cut repetition before cutting the explanation that makes an argument understandable. Cross-references should do actual work, not merely signal familiarity with Scripture.

### 3. Questions sound like a conversation

Jonathan anticipates confusion and objections, then works through them with the reader. “What? How can that be?” and “Surprise, surprise” in [What God Wants](src/posts/c8eab38.md) give the exposition a recognizable spoken quality.

Use questions that advance the argument, rather than decorative questions with predetermined slogans as answers. Allow natural surprise, plain speech, and occasional humor without turning serious matters into jokes.

### 4. Concrete life meets serious theology

Analogies come from recognizable experiences: computers, music, ordinary situations, and popular culture. [Help Me, O My Creator](src/posts/8d3b69d.md) uses the relationship between a computer and its manufacturer to explain dependence on the Creator. [Don't Waste Your Life](src/posts/ca73620.md) connects a biblical warning with familiar cultural illustrations.

An illustration must clarify the passage and return the reader to it. Do not force a technology reference into every post, reuse an old illustration mechanically, or treat an analogy as proof.

### 5. Urgency is personal and affectionate

The writing aims at repentance, worship, faith, prayer, and love—not understanding alone. It can name sin directly while grieving for people and pleading for their restoration or salvation.

[Real Men Cry](src/posts/b7931be.md) joins theological conviction with sorrow and intercession. [I Hastened and Did Not Delay](src/posts/3af64b1.md) moves from exposition to immediate application and prayer. Creator–creature dependence and reverence for God recur throughout these posts.

Keep both conviction and compassion. Let the closing response arise from the passage and the brief; do not bolt on an interchangeable devotional ending.

## Clarity and cadence

- Develop a thought in connected sentences; vary sentence length naturally.
- Use an occasional short, blunt sentence for emphasis. Do not make every paragraph a standalone maxim.
- Avoid repeated “not X, but Y” constructions, symmetrical slogans, and an artificially polished inspirational cadence. A useful contrast is welcome; a template is not.
- Keep direct vocabulary and simple transitions. Explain necessary theological terms.
- Do not reproduce old typos, cumbersome sentences, digressions, or unsupported claims in the name of authenticity.
- For a short post, spend words on Scripture and necessary reasoning rather than repeated reassurance or extra illustrations.

## Scripture and source fidelity

Let Scripture speak, including warnings as well as encouragement. When an argument depends on a verse's contrast, present both halves. Preserve the distinction between quotation, interpretation, illustration, and personal application.

Use the requested translation consistently, retain the required copyright notice, and verify quotations and historical attributions. Never improve an exact quotation to fit the desired voice. An older post is evidence of writing style, not automatic validation of every factual or theological claim it contains.

## Editorial review

Apply SPEAR proportionately: scope the brief and audience; plan the passage-to-application sequence; execute the draft; assess it with the rubric below; resolve by addressing gaps and handing off the reviewed result. This does not authorize merging or publication.

- **Content fidelity:** Does the draft preserve Jonathan's intended argument, source context, and required passages without invented personal details?
- **Reasoning:** Can the reader follow the definitions, questions, and connections that lead to the conclusion?
- **Voice:** Is the author personally accountable to the text, conversational, and recognizably involved rather than a detached devotional narrator?
- **Economy and cadence:** Is the prose clear and appropriately short, with developed thoughts rather than a string of slogans?
- **Response:** Does the application follow from the passage, retaining both truthful confrontation and care for the reader?

If a draft is doctrinally clear but could be attributed to any devotional writer, revisit voice—not just vocabulary. When uncertain, compare it with two or three relevant source posts above rather than treating an earlier AI draft as the standard.
