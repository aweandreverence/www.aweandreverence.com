A Righteous Man Falls Seven Times—and Rises Again

by Jonathan Tsai - September 11, 2026

Tags: faith, righteousness, evangelism, hope, resilience, steadfastness, recovery, perseverance, encouragement, grace, salvation, resurrection

Conceived September 11, 2026; published September 13, 2026.

How do you become unstoppable?

Not by never falling—but by belonging to the God who sustains you.

“For a righteous man falls seven times, and rises again,
But the wicked stumble in time of calamity.”

— Proverbs 24:16

The righteous man falls, but he rises again. What happens to the wicked? We must not stop reading before the warning. Psalm 1 ends with the same contrast:

“For the LORD knows the way of the righteous,
But the way of the wicked will perish.”

— Psalm 1:6

This is not a promise of invulnerability or uninterrupted success. The context is adversity. “Seven times” emphasizes repeated falling, not a limit after which hope expires.

Which of these two ways am I walking? Before claiming the confidence of the righteous, each of us needs to answer that question.

Who is righteous?

We admire honesty. Abraham Lincoln’s reputation earned him the name “Honest Abe.” But a good reputation before people is not the same as right standing before God.

“Who may ascend into the hill of the LORD? And who may stand in His holy place? He who has clean hands and a pure heart…”

— Psalm 24:3–4

God’s standard reaches both our actions and our motives. Who meets it?

“THERE IS NONE RIGHTEOUS, NOT EVEN ONE.”

— Romans 3:10

“for all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God”

— Romans 3:23

None of us can establish right standing with God by our own goodness. So how can anyone be called righteous?

What we cannot earn, Christ provides

“All of us like sheep have gone astray, Each of us has turned to his own way; But the LORD has caused the iniquity of us all To fall on Him.”

— Isaiah 53:6

Jesus Christ, who never sinned, bore our sins on the cross. Through His shed blood, sinners can be forgiven and reconciled to God.

Rock of Ages captures our helplessness:

“Not the labors of my hands
Could fulfill Thy law’s demands”

We receive right standing with God by grace, through faith in Jesus Christ—not by improving ourselves enough to deserve it. His righteousness, not our record, is the ground of our acceptance. (Romans 3:21–26)

Why we can rise again

Christian confidence is not “Nothing can hurt me.” It is “My life rests in the hands of Almighty God.”

David declares:

“The LORD is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear?”

— Psalm 27:1

Jesus Christ rose from the dead. We trust a living Savior, and Because He Lives gives voice to that confidence:

“Because He lives, I can face tomorrow…”

That confidence changes how we live:

“For the love of Christ controls us…”

“and He died for all, so that they who live might no longer live for themselves, but for Him who died and rose again on their behalf.”

— 2 Corinthians 5:14–15

We do not obey to earn acceptance; we obey because, in Christ, we have received it.

Author’s note

September 11 marked the 25th anniversary of the attacks—a day to remember the lives lost and those who still grieve. Recently, I also marked 25 years since God saved me and I began following Him. My confidence is not in my own strength, or even in how strong my faith is. It is in the Almighty God who put the sun, moon, and stars in their places. He is the One I am trusting.

Brothers and sisters, if you feel knocked down, remember your standing in Christ. Bring Him your grief, repent where you have sinned, and take the next faithful step. Your confidence rests in His faithfulness, not your strength.

If you do not know this confidence, I would be glad to share how you can know God personally. Or talk with a Christian you know. Turn from sin and trust the Savior who died and rose again.

Hear God’s invitation—and what Christ has done to make it possible:

“Therefore, we are ambassadors for Christ, as though God were making an appeal through us; we beg you on behalf of Christ, be reconciled to God.

He made Him who knew no sin to be sin on our behalf, so that we might become the righteousness of God in Him.”

— 2 Corinthians 5:20–21
